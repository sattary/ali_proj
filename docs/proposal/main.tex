\begin{abstract}
Phase unwrapping remains a foundational challenge in interferometric imaging, including Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR) and optical metrology. This proposal introduces a novel framework: the Residue-Aware Physics-Informed Swin-UNet. By combining the global context-gathering power of Swin Transformers with explicit topological residue detection and physics-informed loss constraints, we address the critical long-range dependencies and discontinuities that plague traditional convolutional approaches. Initial verification on "stress-test" synthetic datasets demonstrates superior performance in resolving 2$\pi$ ambiguities under high-noise and complex geometric conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As Richard Feynman once famously simplified complex systems, the problem of phase unwrapping is essentially the task of mapping a "bumpy floor" using a measuring stick that only goes from 0 to $2\pi$. The raw measurement—the wrapped phase—is a periodic representation of the true absolute phase. The objective is to reconstruct the continuous surface by resolving these 2$\pi$ jumps.

While traditional algorithms such as SNAPHU (Minimum Cost Flow) provide a robust baseline, they often fail in regions of low coherence or abrupt phase jumps (cliffs). Recent breakthroughs in Deep Learning (DL) offer a path forward, but purely data-driven models often neglect the underlying physics of interferometry. Our proposed framework bridges this gap.

\section{Literature Review: The 2024--2026 Frontier}
The state-of-the-art in phase unwrapping has shifted towards hybrid and unsupervised architectures.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unsupervised Learning:} The \textsl{U$^3$Net} proposed at CVPR 2024 \cite{chen2024u3net} utilizes a recorruption-based self-reconstruction loss in the gradient domain, enabling training without ground-truth absolute phase.
    \item \textbf{Generative Models:} \textsl{UnwrapDiff} (2025) \cite{song2025unwrapdiff} employs Conditional Diffusion Models (DDPM) to correct SNAPHU errors, showing robustness in high-gradient regions.
    \item \textbf{Classification-based Approaches:} \textsl{PhaseNet 2.0} \cite{spoorthi2020phasenet} treats unwrapping as a wrap-count classification problem, though it is often limited by local receptive fields.
    \item \textbf{Gradient Estimation:} Networks like \textsl{PGENet} \cite{pgenet2022} focus on estimating phase gradients directly, which are kemudian solved using least-squares methods.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\subsection{The Swin-UNet Architecture}
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are limited by their local receptive fields. In phase unwrapping, a jump in one corner of the image might be topologically related to a jump in another. To address this, we implement a \textbf{Swin-UNet}. Utilizing shifted-window multi-head self-attention, the model captures global dependencies crucial for resolving large-scale ambiguities.

\subsection{Residue-Aware Topological Detection}
A "residue" in phase unwrapping is a singularity where the sum of phase differences around a $2\times2$ pixel loop is non-zero ($\pm 2\pi$). These points are the "cracks in the sidewalk" of our phase map. Our framework includes a dedicated \textbf{Residue Detection Head}, trained with Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE). By explicitly identifying these singularities, the network can adjust its reconstruction strategy in high-risk zones.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Loss (PIL)}
We do not treat the model as a black box. Instead, we enforce a physical constraint known as the \textsl{Wrapped Gradient Consistency}. The loss function $\mathcal{L}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = w_1 \mathcal{L}_{MAE} + w_2 \mathcal{L}_{Res} + w_3 | W(\nabla \phi_{pred}) - W(\nabla \phi_{input}) |
\end{equation}
where $W$ is the wrapping operator. This ensures that the steepness of the predicted hill matches the measured slopes, even when absolute labels are sparse or noisy.

\section{Data Simulation and Stress Testing}
To validate this proposal, we have developed a "v3 Stress-Test" simulation environment. Unlike standard smooth blobs, our dataset incorporates:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Complex Geometries:} Overlapping cylinders and rectangles with sharp cliff heights.
    \item \textbf{Non-uniform Illumination:} Gaussian beam profiles and occluded "shadow" regions.
    \item \textbf{Realistic Noise:} Multiplicative speckle and additive sensor noise.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
The Residue-Aware Physics-Informed Swin-UNet represents a significant advancement in interferometric phase reconstruction. By marrying the clarity of Feynman-style physical intuition with the power of modern Transformers, we provide a robust solution for next-generation InSAR and metrology applications.

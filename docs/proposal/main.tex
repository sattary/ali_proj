\begin{abstract}
Phase unwrapping is a mathematically ill-posed inverse problem critical to optical interferometry, synthetic aperture radar (SAR), and quantitative phase imaging. Classical methods, including branch-cut and minimum-norm algorithms, are notoriously fragile in the presence of intense optical noise and dense fringe discontinuities. In this proposal, we introduce a fully convolutional deep learning architecture designed for robust, direct absolute phase reconstruction from raw interferograms. We detail a modified U-Net architecture that explicitly integrates normalized spatial coordinate embeddings to resolve global scalar ambiguities. Furthermore, we propose a mathematically rigorous curriculum noise synthesis pipeline that progressively exposes the network to physically accurate optical aberrations, laser speckle, and quantum shot noise. By employing a multi-term loss topology encompassing point-wise absolute error, intensity-weighted Sobel gradient consistency, and discrete Laplacian curvature regularization, we hypothesize that the proposed model will significantly outperform classical approaches in noisy, real-world optical regimes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Optical interferometry relies on the superposition of electromagnetic wavefields to extract high-resolution topographical and structural data. However, the recorded interference pattern encodes the physical phase mathematically wrapped within the principal domain of $[-\pi, \pi)$. Extracting the continuous, absolute physical phase---a process known as phase unwrapping---is an essential but mathematically challenging operation.

Classical phase unwrapping techniques generally fall into two categories: path-following and minimum-norm approaches. Path-following methods struggle catastrophically when noise density breaks the structural integrity of the phase fringes, leading to unresolvable error propagation. Minimum-norm approaches minimize derivative discrepancies but often distort the absolute macroscopic structure.

Recent advancements in deep learning have demonstrated the capability of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to implicitly map the nonlinear relationship between fringe intensity and absolute phase. In this research, we propose a physics-informed deep learning architecture designed to overcome the critical limitations of standard CNNs, specifically the $2\pi k$ translational ambiguity and the lack of physical noise resilience.

\section{Deterministic Synthetic Data Generation and Physical Modeling}

A fundamental vulnerability of deep learning in physical sciences is domain shift caused by insufficient training diversity. To ensure robust generalization to empirical laboratory data while maintaining benchmark integrity, we developed a highly rigorous, deterministic synthetic data generator.

\subsection{The Reproducibility Guarantee}
To generate massive training distributions without compromising scientific benchmarking, the computational pipeline employs a master-seed architecture. A single root cryptographic seed deterministically branches into thousands of completely independent sub-seeds for concurrent multiprocess workers. This mathematically guarantees zero sample overlap or hash collisions across a $100,000+$ image dataset, while ensuring the entire distribution can be perfectly regenerated by a third-party reviewer to validate performance benchmarks.

\subsection{Forward Optical Model}
The synthetic interferograms generated for this study represent the interference of two coherent light beams. To standardize the topological structure of the absolute phase fields, the optical generator strictly utilizes a fixed Helium-Neon laser wavelength of $\lambda = 632.8$ nm. The uniform global scalar multiplier generated by this fixed wavelength forces the network to learn normalized continuous fringe geometries without introducing degenerate scale-invariant solutions.

The primary wavefront $\phi_1(x,y)$ represents randomized structural topography augmented with low-order polynomial aberrations. The reference beam $\phi_2(x,y)$ is mathematically modeled as a divergent spherical wave propagating from a fixed longitudinal focal distance of $Z = 0.5$ m.

Let the base complex amplitude be denoted by $E_0$. The recorded intensity field $I(x,y)$ represents the squared magnitude of the superposition of the two wavefields:
\begin{equation}
    I(x,y) = \left| E_0 e^{i\phi_1(x,y)} + E_0 e^{i\phi_2(x,y)} \right|^2
\end{equation}

\subsection{Supervised Regression Target}
The objective of the model is to map the observed intensity $I(x,y)$ directly to the absolute phase difference between the two beams. To constrain the continuous scalar phase space, the supervised target $\Delta\phi(x,y)$ is shifted to a positive domain:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\phi(x,y) = (\phi_1 - \phi_2) - \min(\phi_1 - \phi_2)
\end{equation}

\section{Physics-Based Dynamic Noise Curriculum}
Neural networks are highly susceptible to domain shift when applied to empirical laboratory data. To close the gap between synthetic data and physical instrumentation, we introduce an extensive optical degradation pipeline applied dynamically during training via a piecewise curriculum schedule $\gamma(\text{epoch}) \in [0,1]$.

The noise synthesis adheres strictly to the physical order of operations within an optical sensing array:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lens Aberration:} Optical defocus is modeled as a Gaussian blur applied to the continuous optical field.
    \item \textbf{Laser Speckle:} Coherent noise arising from micro-scattering is modeled multiplicatively: $I = I \cdot (1 + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{speckle}}))$.
    \item \textbf{Uneven Illumination:} Low-frequency beam misalignment is simulated via the bicubic upsampling of an independent, low-dimensional Gaussian manifold.
    \item \textbf{Sensor Electronics:} Photometric gain and offset jitter simulate variation in camera exposure settings.
    \item \textbf{Quantum Shot Noise:} Photon arrival statistics are strictly enforced via a positive-domain Poisson counting process.
    \item \textbf{Thermal Noise:} Additive Gaussian white noise models dark current and thermal sensor fluctuations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Network Architecture and Spatial Anchoring}

\subsection{Breaking Translational Invariance}
Standard convolutional operations are translationally invariant. However, interferograms represent phase fields modulo $2\pi$, meaning an identical local fringe pattern can represent an infinite number of absolute phase depths (the $2\pi k$ scalar ambiguity). 

To resolve this, our architecture utilizes an \texttt{AddCoords} module, explicitly concatenating normalized spatial grid coordinates $(x,y) \in [-1, 1]^2$ to the input tensor. This breaks the spatial invariance of the U-Net, grounding the predicted manifold and allowing the network to synthesize an absolute scalar offset based on spatial priors.

\subsection{Piston Phase Hinting}
To further regularize the solution manifold and prevent dataset memorization, the network is provided with a one-channel scalar hint $\phi_{\text{hint}}$ derived from the center pixel of the target phase. Crucially, a randomized scalar perturbation $\delta$ is applied to this hint and identically reflected in the ground truth target during the curriculum, mathematically penalizing the network if it fails to lock onto the provided macroscopic anchor.

\section{Multi-Term Topological Loss}
Due to the ill-posed nature of absolute phase unwrapping, optimizing the network weights $\theta$ exclusively via a point-wise absolute error typically fails to capture the high-frequency structural integrity of the continuous optical surface. Consequently, the network is optimized via a multi-objective loss topology $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ that rigorously enforces physical continuity, spatial derivatives, and localized confidence weighting:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{curv}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Global Absolute Accuracy ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}}$)}
The foundation of the optimization is the global $L_1$ norm (Mean Absolute Error), defined over the spatial dimensions $N = H \times W$. This term strictly anchors the unwrapped manifold to the absolute domain to eliminate the macroscopic $2\pi k$ piston shift:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}} = w_{\text{mae}} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{x,y} \left| \phi_{\text{pred}}(x,y) - \phi_{\text{gt}}(x,y) \right|
\end{equation}
While $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}}$ ensures convergence of the bulk surface geometry, it provides insufficient gradient signal to recover sharp, high-frequency topographical fringes, necessitating specialized structural regularization.

\subsection{Intensity-Weighted Gradient Consistency ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}$)}
To enforce high-fidelity structural reconstruction, we compute the spatial depthwise phase derivatives. We approximate the continuous gradients $(\nabla_x, \nabla_y)$ utilizing discrete $3 \times 3$ Sobel convolution kernels, denoted $S_x$ and $S_y$:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_x \phi \approx S_x \ast \phi, \quad \nabla_y \phi \approx S_y \ast \phi
\end{equation}
By penalizing discrepancies in the spatial derivatives, the network is strongly discouraged from generating overly smoothed, "averaged" phase fields that destroy sharp phase discontinuities. 

Furthermore, we introduce a physical confidence weighting mechanism. In optical interferometry, regions of low intensity inherently exhibit low signal-to-noise ratios (SNR). Thus, we mathematically condition the gradient loss on the square root of the normalized raw intensity $I(x,y)$, effectively masking out derivative errors in dark regions while heavily penalizing structural topological deviations in regions of bright interference fringes:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}} = w_{\text{grad}} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{x,y} \sqrt{I(x,y)} \cdot \Big( 
    &\left| \nabla_x\phi_{\text{pred}} - \nabla_x\phi_{\text{gt}} \right| + \nonumber \\
    &\left| \nabla_y\phi_{\text{pred}} - \nabla_y\phi_{\text{gt}} \right| \Big)
\end{align}

\subsection{Laplacian Smoothness Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{curv}}$)}
Convolutional decoding layers within U-Net architectures intrinsically generate high-frequency geometric artifacts (e.g., checkerboard artifacts) that violate the physical continuity of an electromagnetic wavefront. To enforce optical smoothness, we apply an $L_1$ penalty to the unrolled predicted surface utilizing a discrete 2D Laplacian operator. 

Using the finite difference stencil formulation, the second-order spatial derivative at pixel coordinate $(i,j)$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla^2 \phi_{i,j} = \phi_{i+1,j} + \phi_{i-1,j} + \phi_{i,j+1} + \phi_{i,j-1} - 4\phi_{i,j}
\end{equation}
The curvature penalty behaves akin to enforcing rigidity within an elastic membrane, effectively suppressing chaotic phase oscillations unconstrained by the first-order derivative loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{curv}} = w_{\text{curv}} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i,j} \left| \nabla^2 \phi_{\text{pred}}(i,j) \right|
\end{equation}


\section{Evaluation Methodology}
Evaluating absolute phase reconstruction is complex due to global scalar shifts (the "piston" effect). Before calculating final diagnostic metrics (RMSE, MAE, SSIM), the validation pipeline performs a Least-Squares Affine Alignment on the raw prediction:
\begin{equation}
    a \cdot \phi_{\text{pred}} + c \approx \phi_{\text{gt}}
\end{equation}
Metrics are computed on the aligned prediction $\hat{\phi} = a\phi_{\text{pred}} + c$. This explicitly isolates the network's topological unwrapping capability from trivial global drift errors.

\section{Recent Implementation Refinements}
To strictly enforce the mathematical integrity and optical realism of the phase unwrapping pipeline, several core architectural optimizations were recently implemented:

\subsection{Rigorous Optical Pipeline Ordering}
The simulation of physical phenomena was reordered to faithfully match empirical laboratory conditions. Optical aberrations (lens defocus) now strictly precede multiplicative laser speckle, followed by macroscopic beam illumination biases, camera sensor clipping, quantum shot noise, and finally thermal Gaussian additive noise. This prevents physically impossible scenarios, such as optical blur being applied to internal electronic sensor noise.

\subsection{Piston Hinting Consistency}
We identified and corrected a mathematical contradiction in the phase piston anchor mechanism. Previously, the network was provided a scalar phase hint $\phi_{\text{hint}}$ corrupted by a random offset $\delta$ to prevent overfitting, but the regression target $\phi_{\text{gt}}$ lacked this matching translation. This penalized the network for successfully mapping the absolute depth scalar. By directly applying the perturbation $\delta$ to both the input hint and the ground truth target ($\phi_{\text{gt}} \to \phi_{\text{gt}} + \delta$), the network gradient directions are perfectly aligned with the absolute physical phase offset.

\subsection{Intensity-Domain Quantum Shot Noise}
The simulation of photon arrival statistics (Poisson noise) was restructured to operate exclusively within the strictly positive physical intensity domain. This resolved errors where standard normalization (Z-scoring) pushed intensity ranges into negative boundaries, causing non-physical zero-clipping and catastrophic mathematical degradation during subsequent noise augmentations.

\subsection{Fractional Curriculum Scaling}
The dynamic noise curriculum $\gamma(\text{epoch})$ was transitioned from absolute epoch tracking to relative fractional scaling (\texttt{warmup\_ratio} and \texttt{full\_ratio}). This ensures the curriculum function robustly self-scales regardless of the specific training horizon, enabling automated multi-trial hyperparameter optimization runs to perfectly compress and replicate the identical noise scheduling dynamics of massive long-duration convergences.

\section{Conclusion}
By rigidly marrying deep convolutional learning with established optical physics, the proposed pipeline represents a robust methodology for phase unwrapping. The implementation of spatial coordinate embeddings, dynamic optical noise scheduling, and multi-derivative loss topologies ensures that the network is explicitly trained to handle empirical, hardware-degraded interferograms with unprecedented accuracy.
